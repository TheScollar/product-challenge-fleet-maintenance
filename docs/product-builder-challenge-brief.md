# Product Builder Challenge

**Fleet Maintenance**

MARKT-PILOT

---

## The challenge

Build a working prototype of a product that helps a company manage maintenance for a fleet of vehicles.

Imagine a company operating around 40 to 50 delivery vans. At any given time, some vehicles may require routine servicing, have upcoming inspections, show warning signs, need repairs, or have maintenance that could potentially be postponed.

The company wants to keep its vehicles available and reliable while avoiding unnecessary maintenance, unexpected breakdowns, and operational disruption.

**What the product actually does is intentionally underspecified.**

Fleet maintenance could involve monitoring vehicle condition, identifying problems, prioritizing work, planning maintenance, coordinating with garages, understanding costs, keeping service history, or helping someone decide what should happen next.

**You are not expected to solve all of these problems.**

Decide who your primary user is, what you believe their most important problem is, and design a coherent journey around it.

Build enough of the product that we can actually use the primary journey.

You can assume that vehicle data, service history, sensor information, garage availability, prices, and other external information can be obtained through APIs. Feel free to mock whatever data or integrations you need.

For example, the available information for a vehicle might include things such as:

- Mileage
- Vehicle age
- Last service
- Upcoming inspection date
- Reported or detected problems
- Maintenance history
- Estimated repair or service cost
- How important the vehicle is to current operations

You are free to decide which of these matter to your product.

---

## What we're interested in

### We care primarily about:

- how you frame the problem;
- what you choose to build;
- what you deliberately leave out;
- the order in which the user encounters things;
- how you help the user understand what deserves attention;
- how much complexity you expose to the user;
- whether the resulting experience helps someone make decisions rather than simply presenting information;
- whether the result feels like a coherent product rather than a collection of features

> **The visual polish of the prototype matters less than the quality of the product thinking behind it.**
>
> Be prepared to walk us through the decisions you made, alternatives you considered, and what you would build next.
